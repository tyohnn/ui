# Reading a visual reference

Describe a reference by measuring it, not by naming its style. Look at one screen at a time and write numbers.

| Axis | Look at | Write down |
|---|---|---|
| Colour | page background, card background, text, the one colour on the primary button, borders | five colours as hex or oklch; how far the card is from the page (none, a tint, a step) |
| Density | a button's height, a list row's height, padding inside a card, the gap between cards | px, estimated against the text size (body text is usually 14 to 16px) |
| Depth | shadow under cards and menus: none, a 1px ring, soft and wide, layered | the closest of flat / hairline / soft / lifted, and whether popups lift more than cards |
| Texture | corner radius of buttons, inputs, cards; border width; blur behind panels; gradients or sheen | radii in px; border 0 / 1 / 2px; blur yes or no |
| Typography | the font's family (serif, grotesk, geometric, mono), weight of buttons and titles, letter case of labels, tracking of titles | family kind, weights, uppercase or not, tight or loose |

Then write the concept from those numbers. A screenshot is evidence for the axes it shows; do not invent an axis it
does not show (a landing page shows little about rows and menus: take density from the mood instead and say so).

Several references that disagree: take each axis from the reference that shows it best, and say which.

Never copy a brand's logo, name or exact palette as the system's identity. Take the feel; the colours become the
person's own.
